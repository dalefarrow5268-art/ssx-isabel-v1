# SSX Master Change Management Activities

Potential construction schedule activities for identifying, evaluating, pricing, approving, implementing and closing changes from design development through warranty.

> Use only the activities required by the contract, project procedures and actual change. A field direction, RFI response or proposed change should not automatically be treated as authorization to proceed when formal approval is required.

## Core change questions

Every change should answer:

| Field | Required answer |
|---|---|
| What changed? | Scope, design, quantity, quality, method, sequence, access, duration, procurement, system or acceptance requirement |
| Why? | Owner request, design development, conflict, omission, code/AHJ requirement, unforeseen condition, substitution, value engineering, quality issue or contractor proposal |
| Where? | Project, building, floor, room, grid, elevation, system, equipment tag or work package |
| When discovered? | Date, schedule update period and construction status |
| Who initiated it? | Owner, architect, engineer, contractor, subcontractor, supplier, utility, AHJ or commissioning authority |
| What is affected? | Cost, time, procurement, labor, safety, quality, permits, testing, commissioning, turnover or warranty |
| What blocks work? | Decision, design, pricing, authorization, material, permit, inspection or access |
| Who approves? | Contract-defined decision maker and required technical reviewers |
| What is the need-by date? | Latest date that avoids or limits delay |
| What evidence closes it? | Executed change, revised documents, installed-work verification, testing, record documents and cost/schedule reconciliation |

## Standard change-management workflow

1. Identify potential change
2. Protect people, property and completed work
3. Document existing conditions
4. Notify required parties
5. Assign change ID
6. Confirm contract notice requirements
7. Define scope and exact location
8. Determine whether work may continue
9. Issue interim direction when authorized
10. Develop design information
11. Identify affected trades and suppliers
12. Request itemized pricing
13. Request schedule-impact analysis
14. Review constructability and coordination
15. Review procurement and long-lead effects
16. Review permits, inspections and commissioning effects
17. Reconcile scope, cost and time
18. Obtain technical approvals
19. Obtain owner/funding approval
20. Execute formal authorization
21. Issue revised documents
22. Integrate change into schedule and cost controls
23. Procure changed materials
24. Implement changed work
25. Inspect, test and commission
26. Update record documents
27. Reconcile final cost and time
28. Close change and preserve audit trail

## Typical schedule logic

- Potential change identified → notice and investigation
- Investigation → scope definition
- Scope definition → design and proposal documents
- Proposal documents → pricing and schedule analysis
- Pricing and analysis → review and negotiation
- Reconciled proposal → owner decision
- Approval → revised documents and procurement
- Procurement/design release → field implementation
- Installation → inspection, testing and commissioning
- Acceptance → cost/time reconciliation
- Reconciliation and records → change closure

## Required schedule controls

- Give every potential change a unique ID.
- Calculate a decision need-by date from the affected work, not an arbitrary response date.
- Connect each change to the exact blocked or affected schedule activities.
- Separate potential, submitted, approved and implemented values.
- Do not treat pricing approval as field authorization unless the contract permits it.
- Preserve contractual notices and contemporaneous records.
- Track design, pricing, schedule review and owner approval separately.
- Include procurement, permitting, testing, commissioning and closeout impacts.
- Show mitigation and acceleration as explicit activities.
- Update field documents immediately after authorization.
- Close a change only after work, records, cost and time are reconciled.
