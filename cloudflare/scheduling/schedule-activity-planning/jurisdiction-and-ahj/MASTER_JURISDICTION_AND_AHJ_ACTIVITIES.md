# SSX Master Construction Jurisdiction and AHJ Activities

Potential planning, zoning, permitting, code-review, inspection, enforcement, acceptance, occupancy and closeout activities performed by authorities having jurisdiction on a U.S. construction project.

> Authorities, terminology, adopted codes, review sequences and inspection requirements vary by location and project. Confirm all requirements with the actual federal, state, county, municipal, tribal, territorial, special-district and delegated authorities governing the work.

## Recommended SSX tracking fields

For every applicable AHJ activity, track:

- Authority and department
- Jurisdiction level
- Reviewer, inspector and contact
- Application, permit, case and inspection number
- Project, phase, building, floor, area and system
- Activity and review classification
- Governing code, ordinance, standard and adopted edition
- Required application, drawings, calculations and certifications
- Required licensed professional or contractor
- Submission prerequisites
- Public notice, hearing and board dates
- Planned, forecast and actual submission dates
- Completeness, review and response durations
- Inspection request and notice period
- Planned and actual inspection dates
- Result, comments and corrections
- Reinspection and approval dates
- Fees, bonds, deposits and penalties
- Conditions of approval
- Expiration, renewal and closeout dates
- Linked permit, utility, construction and occupancy activities
- Whether it blocks work, concealment, activation, occupancy or payment
- Final approval record and attachment

## Standard AHJ workflow

1. Identify every authority having jurisdiction
2. Confirm adopted codes and local amendments
3. Confirm required approvals, permits and inspections
4. Establish AHJ contacts
5. Hold pre-application meeting when appropriate
6. Confirm submission forms and requirements
7. Prepare and submit application
8. Pay review fees
9. Complete administrative completeness review
10. Respond to completeness comments
11. Complete technical review
12. Coordinate parallel department reviews
13. Receive review comments
14. Prepare response matrix
15. Revise and resubmit
16. Attend hearing, board or code meeting when required
17. Obtain conditional or final approval
18. Satisfy pre-issuance conditions
19. Pay permit fees and post bonds
20. Receive permit and approved plans
21. Hold preconstruction meeting
22. Request progressive inspections
23. Correct deficiencies and obtain reinspections
24. Submit revisions and deferred submittals
25. Complete acceptance testing
26. Complete trade and agency final inspections
27. Obtain Fire Marshal and building finals
28. Obtain TCO, CO and operating approvals
29. Close permits and release bonds
30. Archive approved records

## Typical schedule logic

- Jurisdiction research → pre-application meeting
- Pre-application → permit documents
- Application → completeness review
- Completeness approval → technical review
- Review comments → revision and resubmission
- Approval → permit issuance
- Permit issuance → authorized work
- Construction phase → progressive inspections
- Inspection pass → concealment or successor work
- System completion → acceptance testing
- Trade finals → building and Fire Marshal finals
- Final approvals → TCO or CO
- Stabilization and records → permit closeout
- Closeout → bond and deposit release

## Common hold points

- No regulated work before permit issuance
- No land disturbance before environmental authorization
- No concealment before required inspection
- No utility activation before required releases
- No life-safety system use before acceptance
- No equipment operation before required certificate
- No occupancy before TCO or CO
- No final permit closeout before reports and violations are resolved
- No bond release before required maintenance and final acceptance
- No assumption that one authority’s approval replaces another’s

## Required schedule controls

- Identify every authority early.
- Confirm current codes, amendments, forms and review durations.
- Separate administrative review, technical review, response and resubmission.
- Schedule hearings and board deadlines explicitly.
- Link progressive inspections to concealment and successor work.
- Include failed-inspection correction and reinspection time.
- Track Fire Marshal activities independently from building-department activities.
- Tie TCO and CO to every required authority and certificate.
- Track permit expiration, TCO conditions and operational-license renewals.
- Preserve final approvals and stamped documents.
- Do not treat submission as approval or one department’s approval as final jurisdictional acceptance.
