# SSX Master Construction Meetings and Coordination Activities

Potential project, design, field, trade, safety, quality, procurement, commissioning, owner, utility, AHJ and closeout meetings or coordination activities that may affect a construction schedule.

> Include only meetings that serve a defined project need. Every scheduled meeting should have an owner, required participants, agenda, decisions, action items, due dates and a clear relationship to the work.

## Recommended SSX tracking fields

For every applicable meeting or coordination activity, track:

- Project, phase, building, floor, area and system
- Meeting name, type and purpose
- Required and optional participants
- Chair, facilitator and minute taker
- Recurrence, day, time, duration and location
- Planned and actual date
- Required notice period
- Agenda issue deadline
- Required pre-read and supporting documents
- Decisions needed
- Schedule activities and milestones affected
- Open RFIs, submittals, changes, risks and constraints
- Action-item owner and due date
- Decision authority
- Escalation level and deadline
- Minutes issue date
- Acceptance or correction deadline for minutes
- Linked drawings, photos, reports and attachments
- Status, result and next meeting
- Whether the meeting is a hold point for work

## Standard meeting workflow

1. Define meeting purpose and desired decisions
2. Identify chair and required participants
3. Establish recurrence or target date
4. Issue calendar invitation and notice
5. Collect agenda topics
6. Review previous minutes and open actions
7. Assemble pre-read documents
8. Issue agenda
9. Conduct meeting
10. Confirm attendance and quorum when required
11. Review safety and urgent constraints
12. Review schedule, work completed and upcoming work
13. Resolve agenda items or assign actions
14. Record decisions, dissent and unresolved issues
15. Assign one owner and due date to each action
16. Confirm escalation path
17. Confirm next meeting
18. Issue draft or final minutes
19. Correct minutes within the defined review period
20. Distribute approved record
21. Update logs, schedule and responsible parties
22. Verify action completion
23. Carry unresolved items forward
24. Close meeting series when no longer needed

## Minutes and action-item controls

- Record date, time, location and attendees
- Identify absent required participants
- Record factual discussion concisely
- Record decisions and approving authority
- Record rejected options when material
- Assign one accountable owner per action
- Assign a specific due date
- Link action to affected schedule activity
- Carry forward unresolved actions
- Mark completed actions with evidence
- Record notice of potential delay
- Record disagreement or requested correction
- Issue minutes within the contract period
- Preserve revisions and final record
- Distribute to all affected parties
- Update RFI, submittal, change, risk and schedule logs

## Decision coordination workflow

1. Define decision required
2. Identify decision authority
3. Identify required supporting information
4. Identify alternatives and recommendation
5. Quantify cost, schedule, quality and risk impacts
6. Establish need-by date
7. Circulate pre-read
8. Conduct decision meeting
9. Record decision or deferral
10. Assign follow-up actions
11. Update design and contract documents
12. Update procurement and schedule
13. Notify affected parties
14. Verify implementation
15. Close decision item

## Required schedule controls

- Include only meetings that create decisions, coordination, commitments or required records.
- Tie phase-specific meetings to the work they enable.
- Give every action one owner and a date.
- Tie overdue actions to affected schedule activities.
- Record decision need-by dates before they become critical.
- Do not treat discussion as approval.
- Escalate unresolved issues at a defined threshold.
- Issue minutes within the contract requirement.
- Preserve meeting records and corrections.
- Close meeting series when their purpose is complete.
