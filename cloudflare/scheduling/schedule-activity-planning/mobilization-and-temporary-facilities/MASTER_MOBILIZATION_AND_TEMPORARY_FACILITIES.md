# SSX Master Construction Mobilization and Temporary Facilities

Potential pre-mobilization, site-establishment, temporary-utility, access, protection, logistics, operations, maintenance, relocation and demobilization activities that may affect a construction project schedule.

> Use only activities appropriate to the contract, site logistics plan, permits, owner requirements, project phasing, workforce, weather, occupied conditions, means and methods, and actual project risks.

## Recommended SSX tracking fields

For each applicable activity, track:

- Project phase, building, floor, area and logistics zone
- Activity and temporary facility type
- Responsible contractor, subcontractor, vendor and person
- Owner, landlord, utility or AHJ dependency
- Required permit, approval, inspection or license
- Predecessors and successors
- Required-on-site and removal dates
- Planned and actual start and finish
- Installation, relocation, maintenance and removal duration
- Capacity, quantity and service level
- Rental start, minimum term and off-rent date
- Delivery, crane, rigging and access requirements
- Utility source, metering and operating cost
- Inspection and preventive-maintenance frequency
- Safety, security, environmental and accessibility requirements
- Phased relocation or expansion requirements
- Status, deficiency and corrective action
- Cost code and responsible payer
- Closeout, restoration and evidence required
- Linked permit, procurement, inspection and schedule activities

## Standard mobilization workflow

1. Confirm notice to proceed and site possession
2. Confirm contract mobilization requirements
3. Verify permits, insurance, bonds and licenses
4. Complete existing-condition documentation
5. Approve site logistics and temporary-facilities plans
6. Confirm utility availability and connection points
7. Confirm site access, delivery routes and work hours
8. Establish survey control and project limits
9. Install environmental and public-protection controls
10. Establish secure perimeter and controlled access
11. Install temporary roads, entrances and laydown areas
12. Deliver and install field offices and support facilities
13. Establish temporary power, lighting, water, sanitation and communications
14. Establish emergency, fire and first-aid systems
15. Establish waste, recycling and housekeeping operations
16. Inspect and approve temporary facilities
17. Mobilize staff, workforce, equipment and initial materials
18. Begin recurring inspection, maintenance and service
19. Expand, relocate or reduce facilities as phases change
20. Transition temporary systems to permanent systems when authorized
21. Demobilize unused facilities
22. Remove temporary utilities and structures
23. Restore disturbed areas
24. Complete final inspection and release
25. Close rentals, permits, accounts and temporary easements

## Typical schedule logic

- Notice to proceed → pre-mobilization planning
- Approved logistics plan → site establishment
- Environmental controls → land disturbance
- Fencing and access → workforce mobilization
- Temporary utilities → offices and field operations
- Temporary roads and laydown → major deliveries
- Crane foundation → crane erection
- Hoist installation → vertical construction
- Temporary enclosure → interior conditioning
- Temporary heat and dry-in → finishes
- Permanent utility acceptance → temporary utility removal
- Permanent life safety → temporary-system removal
- Substantial completion → major demobilization
- Final acceptance → complete restoration and closeout

## Common hold points

- No mobilization before notice to proceed and site access
- No land disturbance before environmental controls and permit
- No workforce access before secure perimeter and orientation
- No temporary utility use before inspection and approval
- No crane or hoist use before certification
- No occupied-area work before approved protection plan
- No removal of temporary fire protection before permanent coverage is active
- No removal of temporary power before permanent service supports the work
- No demobilization that impairs punch, testing, commissioning or warranty work
- No permit or rental closure before final restoration is verified

## Required schedule controls

- Tie each temporary facility to the first activity needing it and the last activity depending on it.
- Include permitting, procurement, delivery, installation, inspection and removal.
- Track recurring maintenance and service when failure could stop work.
- Adjust capacities to workforce and phase changes.
- Coordinate rental dates with actual delivery and off-rent confirmation.
- Preserve emergency access, egress and fire protection through every phase.
- Do not remove temporary systems before permanent replacements are tested and accepted.
- Link environmental controls to stabilization and permit closeout.
- Include final restoration and damage correction.
- Use separate activities for material relocations that affect production.
- Update the logistics plan whenever access, cranes, hoists, barriers or occupied areas change.
