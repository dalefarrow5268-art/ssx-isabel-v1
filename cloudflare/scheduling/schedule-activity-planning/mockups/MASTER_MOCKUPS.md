# SSX Master Construction Mockups

Potential visual, workmanship, coordination, performance, laboratory, field, room, system and prototype mockups that may affect a construction project schedule.

> Include only mockups required by the contract documents, approved submittals, owner, design team, commissioning plan, authority having jurisdiction, manufacturer, warranty provider or project quality plan.

## Recommended SSX tracking fields

For every applicable mockup, track:

- Project, phase, building, floor, area and system
- Mockup name, type and purpose
- CSI divisions and specification sections
- Responsible contractor, trades, suppliers and manufacturers
- Architect, engineer, consultant, owner and AHJ reviewers
- Required submittals, samples, calculations and approvals
- Location, dimensions and configuration
- Permanent, freestanding, laboratory or off-site status
- Materials, products, finishes and approved substitutions
- Required weathering, curing or conditioning time
- Required visual, dimensional, functional or performance tests
- Testing agency, standards, pressures, loads and acceptance criteria
- Planned and actual construction dates
- Planned and actual review and testing dates
- Deficiency, correction and retest dates
- Final approval date
- Production-release date
- Required preservation period
- Removal and restoration dates
- Cost responsibility
- Linked procurement, submittal, testing and installation activities
- Status, disposition and record attachments

## Standard mockup workflow

1. Identify contract-required mockup
2. Define mockup purpose and acceptance criteria
3. Identify all affected trades and reviewers
4. Select mockup type and location
5. Establish required completion and production-release dates
6. Back-calculate design, submittal, procurement and construction dates
7. Prepare mockup plan, drawings and testing procedures
8. Submit mockup plan
9. Complete design-team, owner and consultant review
10. Obtain required permits or laboratory booking
11. Procure approved materials and components
12. Prepare substrate, foundation or test chamber
13. Conduct preconstruction or pre-installation conference
14. Construct mockup in the required sequence
15. Document concealed conditions
16. Complete contractor quality review
17. Correct obvious deficiencies before formal review
18. Conduct architect, engineer, consultant and owner review
19. Conduct performance testing when required
20. Document results and deficiencies
21. Repair, modify or reconstruct
22. Retest
23. Obtain final written approval
24. Record accepted materials, colors, details and workmanship
25. Preserve mockup as the quality benchmark
26. Release production procurement, fabrication or installation
27. Compare initial and ongoing production work to benchmark
28. Remove mockup only after written authorization
29. Restore location
30. Archive photos, reports and approval records

## Typical schedule logic

- Design information → mockup plan
- Mockup plan → submittal approval
- Approval → mockup procurement
- Procurement → mockup construction
- Construction → cure or conditioning
- Cure → visual review
- Visual review → performance testing
- Failed test → correction and retest
- Final approval → production release
- Production installation → benchmark comparisons
- Related work acceptance → mockup removal
- Removal → site restoration

## Common hold points

- No mockup construction before approved plan and materials
- No concealed work before required documentation or inspection
- No performance test before required cure or conditioning
- No production release before written mockup approval
- No unapproved material or detail changes after benchmark approval
- No mockup removal before related work is accepted
- No substitution of digital review for required physical testing
- No failed mockup incorporated into permanent work without approved correction

## Required schedule controls

- Schedule mockups early enough to influence production work.
- Tie owner selections and submittals to mockup procurement.
- Separate construction, cure, review, testing, correction and approval.
- Link final approval directly to fabrication or installation release.
- Include realistic retest contingency for performance mockups.
- Track mockups by system, location and acceptance authority.
- Record the precise approved materials, details and workmanship.
- Preserve approved benchmarks until related work is accepted.
- Apply lessons from mockup failures to production documents and crews.
- Do not allow production to outrun required mockup approval.
