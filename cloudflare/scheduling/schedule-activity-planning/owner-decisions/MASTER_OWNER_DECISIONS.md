# SSX Master Construction Owner Decisions

Potential owner, developer, operator, tenant, user-group, lender, investor and asset-management decisions that may affect design, permits, procurement, construction, commissioning, occupancy or closeout.

> Use only decisions applicable to the project. Every owner decision should identify a single decision authority, required supporting information, cost and schedule consequences, and a need-by date tied to the first affected downstream activity.

## Recommended SSX tracking fields

For every applicable decision, track:

- Project, phase, building, floor, area, room and system
- Decision title, category and description
- Decision number and revision
- Requestor and coordinator
- Decision authority and required approvers
- Consulted stakeholders
- Required design, cost, schedule and operational information
- Options and recommended option
- Budget, contingency and funding impact
- Schedule activities and milestones affected
- Required decision date
- Forecast decision date
- Actual decision date
- Float and days overdue
- Consequence if late
- Escalation date and level
- Decision status and disposition
- Conditions, qualifications and follow-up actions
- Linked RFI, submittal, change, allowance, procurement and drawing
- Required written authorization
- Final decision record and attachment

## Standard owner-decision workflow

1. Identify decision required
2. Define the exact question
3. Identify decision authority
4. Identify consulted stakeholders
5. Identify affected design, procurement and construction
6. Determine the first downstream need date
7. Back-calculate review and decision deadline
8. Gather supporting information
9. Develop feasible options
10. Quantify cost, schedule, quality, risk and operational impacts
11. Provide recommendation
12. Issue decision request
13. Conduct review meeting when required
14. Answer questions and provide supplemental information
15. Obtain formal decision
16. Record conditions and qualifications
17. Obtain change authorization when required
18. Update drawings, specifications and models
19. Update submittals and procurement
20. Update budget and schedule
21. Notify affected parties
22. Verify implementation
23. Close decision item

## Decision need-by calculation

Calculate the owner decision date backward from the earliest affected activity:

1. Installation or occupancy date
2. Delivery and site-logistics duration
3. Shipping duration
4. Fabrication duration
5. Vendor engineering duration
6. Approved-submittal requirement
7. Design-team review duration
8. Contractor review duration
9. Sample or mockup duration
10. Owner evaluation and decision duration
11. Contingency for revision or rejection

A decision is late when it consumes planned review, procurement or construction float—not only when field work stops.

## Escalation workflow

1. Issue upcoming-decision notice
2. Confirm required information is complete
3. Issue decision request
4. Send reminder before need date
5. Mark at risk
6. Quantify current schedule impact
7. Escalate to authorized owner representative
8. Escalate to executive decision authority
9. Document direction or lack of direction
10. Implement authorized mitigation
11. Preserve notice and cost records
12. Update schedule and decision log

- Program decision → design
- Design decision → permit documents
- Finish selection → submittal and mockup
- Mockup approval → production release
- Equipment selection → utility coordination
- Owner-furnished equipment decision → rough-in
- Substitution decision → procurement
- Change authorization → changed work
- TCO decision → phased occupancy plan
- Training decision → turnover
- Acceptance decision → substantial or final completion

## Common hold points

- No design freeze before program and system decisions
- No permit submission before required owner approvals
- No custom material release before finish selection
- No fabrication before approved configuration
- No change work without proper authorization
- No owner-furnished rough-in before final equipment data
- No mockup production release before written acceptance
- No occupancy before authorized TCO or CO
- No risk transfer without explicit written acceptance
- No final turnover before owner acceptance requirements are met

## Required schedule controls

- Assign every decision a true need-by date.
- Tie the need-by date to the first affected downstream activity.
- Identify one authorized decision authority.
- Provide complete options and impacts before requesting a decision.
- Separate recommendation, approval and change authorization.
- Track owner decisions independently from RFIs and submittals.
- Record decisions in writing.
- Update design, procurement, budget and schedule immediately.
- Escalate before float is exhausted.
- Preserve superseded decisions and revision history.
- Verify the decision was implemented.
- Do not assume silence means approval.
