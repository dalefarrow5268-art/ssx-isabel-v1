# SSX Master Construction Permits and Licenses

Potential governmental, utility, environmental, professional, trade, operational and occupancy authorizations that may affect a U.S. construction project schedule.

> Requirements vary by state, county, municipality, authority having jurisdiction, occupancy, construction type, project funding, site conditions and scope. Use only applicable activities and verify every requirement with the governing authority, design professionals, owner, utilities and contract documents.

## Recommended SSX tracking fields

For every applicable permit, license or authorization, track:

- Project, jurisdiction and issuing authority
- Permit or license name and classification
- Application, permit and revision number
- Related building, phase, area, system and CSI division
- Applicant, permit holder and responsible person
- Required preparer, signer, licensed professional or contractor
- Governing code, ordinance, statute or contract requirement
- Submission prerequisites
- Required drawings, calculations, reports and certifications
- Fees, bonds, deposits and insurance
- Planned and actual application dates
- Completeness-review duration
- Technical-review duration
- Comment-response duration
- Resubmission and approval dates
- Required public notice, hearing or board dates
- Issuance date
- Effective date and expiration date
- Renewal or extension deadline
- Required inspections and closeout documents
- Work authorized and work prohibited
- Conditions of approval
- Posting and jobsite-copy requirements
- Status, aging and escalation owner
- Linked submittals, procurement, inspections and schedule activities
- Whether it blocks mobilization, work, utility activation, occupancy or closeout

## Standard permit workflow

1. Identify applicable authority and authorization
2. Confirm permit or license classification
3. Hold pre-application or pre-permit meeting when appropriate
4. Confirm submission requirements and current forms
5. Confirm design completion and prerequisite approvals
6. Assign applicant and responsible permit manager
7. Prepare application package
8. Obtain required signatures and professional seals
9. Obtain owner authorization
10. Submit application
11. Pay application and review fees
12. Complete administrative completeness review
13. Respond to completeness comments
14. Enter technical review
15. Coordinate parallel agency reviews
16. Receive review comments
17. Prepare comment-response matrix
18. Revise drawings, calculations and documents
19. Resubmit
20. Complete additional review cycles
21. Obtain planning, zoning, board or public-hearing approval when required
22. Obtain conditional approval
23. Satisfy pre-issuance conditions
24. Pay permit, impact and connection fees
25. Post bond, deposit or insurance
26. Receive permit or license
27. Verify authorized scope and conditions
28. Post permit and approved plans onsite
29. Schedule prerequisite and progressive inspections
30. Submit revisions, deferred submittals and supplemental permits
31. Renew or extend before expiration
32. Complete final inspections
33. Submit closeout records and certifications
34. Obtain final acceptance, TCO, CO or operational license
35. Close permit and release bonds or deposits
36. Archive final permit record

## Typical permit schedule logic

- Design criteria → permit drawings
- Permit drawings → application
- Application → completeness review
- Completeness acceptance → technical review
- Review comments → design revisions
- Design revisions → resubmission
- Approval → fee payment and issuance
- Issuance → mobilization or permitted work
- Permitted work → progressive inspections
- Progressive inspections → trade finals
- Trade finals → building and Fire Marshal finals
- Final approvals → TCO or CO
- Stabilization and record documents → permit closeout
- Closeout → bond and deposit release

## Common permit hold points

- No land disturbance before environmental and grading authorization
- No demolition before permit and utility disconnect releases
- No work in public right-of-way before approved traffic control and right-of-way permit
- No foundation work before foundation or building permit
- No trade work before required trade permit
- No concealment before required inspection
- No utility connection before utility authorization
- No energization before electrical and utility release
- No system operation before required certificate or operational permit
- No occupancy before TCO or CO
- No final closeout before all permits and violations are closed

## Required schedule controls

- Verify requirements directly with the current governing authority.
- Use realistic agency durations and required hearing dates.
- Separate application, review, comment response, resubmission and issuance.
- Link permit dates to the first activity legally requiring authorization.
- Track fees, bonds and prerequisite approvals as schedule activities when they can delay issuance.
- Track revisions and deferred submittals separately from the master permit.
- Flag expirations and renewals well before their deadlines.
- Preserve conditions of approval and link them to responsible activities.
- Do not treat submission as approval or approval as issuance.
- Do not treat permit issuance as authorization for work outside its stated scope.
- Confirm field crews use the current stamped plans.
- Verify every permit is closed before final project closeout.
