# SSX Master Construction Procurement and Long-Lead Milestones

Potential planning, sourcing, approval, purchasing, fabrication, logistics, delivery, storage, installation-release and recovery activities that may affect a construction project schedule.

> Apply only the activities appropriate to the project’s contract, procurement method, drawings, specifications, approved submittals, trade packages, owner requirements, market conditions, logistics plan and actual supplier commitments.

## Recommended SSX tracking fields

For each applicable procurement item, track:

- Project, building, phase, floor, area and system
- CSI division, trade and specification section
- Procurement item, equipment tag and package number
- Responsible contractor, subcontractor, vendor, manufacturer and contact
- Owner-furnished, contractor-furnished or utility-furnished status
- Required-on-site, installation and startup dates
- Design-information need date
- Owner-selection need date
- Submittal preparation, review and approval dates
- Purchase-order and release dates
- Quoted, contractual and current lead time
- Engineering, fabrication and assembly durations
- Factory test, inspection and witness requirements
- Shipping origin, freight method, customs and transit duration
- Delivery appointment, crane, street closure and unloading requirements
- Storage, protection and environmental requirements
- Planned, promised, forecast and actual dates
- Critical-path and long-lead flags
- Cost, escalation, deposit and payment milestones
- Status, aging, variance and recovery action
- Linked submittal, RFI, change, delivery and installation activities
- Risk owner and last confirmation date
- Documentation, tracking number, packing list and receiving report

## Standard procurement workflow

1. Identify required material, equipment or service
2. Add item to procurement register
3. Assign responsible trade and procurement owner
4. Confirm basis of design and contract requirement
5. Confirm quantities, locations, tags and interfaces
6. Determine required-on-site and installation dates
7. Establish approved float and contingency
8. Back-calculate delivery, fabrication, approval and decision deadlines
9. Solicit quotations
10. Confirm bidder scope and exclusions
11. Perform technical and commercial comparison
12. Resolve clarifications, alternates and substitutions
13. Select supplier or manufacturer
14. Obtain owner or design-team approval when required
15. Issue letter of intent, early-release authorization or purchase order
16. Obtain vendor acknowledgment
17. Confirm price, terms, quantity, lead time and promised date
18. Prepare and submit product data, shop drawings, samples or calculations
19. Complete internal and design-team review
20. Revise and resubmit when required
21. Obtain approval for procurement or fabrication
22. Release for engineering
23. Complete vendor engineering and field verification
24. Approve final dimensions, finishes and options
25. Release for fabrication
26. Procure raw materials and components
27. Fabricate, assemble and perform internal quality control
28. Conduct source inspection or factory acceptance testing
29. Correct factory deficiencies
30. Approve for shipment
31. Book freight and delivery appointment
32. Complete export, import and customs requirements when applicable
33. Track shipment in transit
34. Coordinate site access, crane, unloading and laydown
35. Receive, count and inspect
36. Document damage, shortage or nonconformance
37. Accept, reject or quarantine material
38. Store and protect
39. Release item to installation
40. Install, connect and complete field quality control
41. Start up, test and commission
42. Deliver spares, special tools, warranties and closeout records
43. Close procurement item

## Common procurement hold points

- No purchase before commercial authorization
- No release before approved technical submittal
- No fabrication before final field dimensions
- No custom finish production before owner approval
- No shipment before factory acceptance
- No delivery before site and storage readiness
- No installation before receiving acceptance
- No energization before AHJ or utility release
- No startup before completed utilities and pre-functional checks
- No final payment before closeout, warranty and spare-parts delivery

- Receiving → installation

## Required schedule controls

- Back-calculate procurement activities from the installation need date.
- Use supplier-confirmed durations, not generic lead-time assumptions, when commitments are available.
- Separate design, approval, manufacturing and shipping durations.
- Record both contractual and current forecast dates.
- Reconfirm long-lead commitments at least weekly.
- Tie critical submittals to purchase, fabrication and delivery milestones.
- Add contingency for approvals, manufacturing risk, freight and site readiness.
- Track partial releases and split shipments separately.
- Do not mark an item delivered until quantity and condition are verified.
- Preserve evidence of vendor commitments and changes.
- Escalate forecast delays before they affect critical-path installation.
- Coordinate procurement status with cost, submittal, delivery and field schedules.
