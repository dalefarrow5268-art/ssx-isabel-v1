# SSX Master Construction Quality Control Activities

Potential planning, review, verification, inspection, testing, documentation, deficiency, corrective-action and acceptance activities used to control construction quality from design and procurement through installation, commissioning and warranty.

> Apply only activities required by the contract documents, project quality plan, approved submittals, codes, manufacturers, authorities, testing agencies, commissioning plan and owner requirements. Quality control is the contractor’s process for producing conforming work; quality assurance independently monitors or verifies that process.

## Core SSX question set: When, Where, How, Who and Evidence

Every quality-control activity should answer:

| Field | Required answer |
|---|---|
| When | Before work, at delivery, at first installation, continuously, at defined frequency, before concealment, at completion, during warranty or after a triggering event |
| Where | Fabrication shop, supplier, warehouse, laydown area, site, building, floor, room, elevation, grid, system, equipment tag, test area or representative sample |
| How | Document review, visual inspection, measurement, mockup, checklist, hold point, witness point, field test, laboratory test, functional test, sampling or digital verification |
| Who | Subcontractor, superintendent, QC manager, trade foreman, testing agency, special inspector, manufacturer, design professional, commissioning authority, owner or AHJ |
| Acceptance | Drawing, specification, approved submittal, code, standard, manufacturer instruction, mockup or measurable tolerance |
| Evidence | Checklist, report, test result, photo, video, survey, certificate, calibration record, marked drawing, deficiency record or signed acceptance |
| Next action | Release work, continue with monitoring, correct, reject, quarantine, retest, reinspect, notify or escalate |

## Three-phase control system

The preparatory, initial and follow-up framework may be used for each definable feature of work. It is a core construction quality-management approach in current U.S. Army Corps of Engineers guidance and is useful beyond federal work when adapted to the contract.

### Preparatory phase — when, where and how

**When:** Before starting each definable feature of work; repeat after major design changes, long interruptions, new subcontractors or crews, failed quality performance or changed conditions.

**Where:** At the workface whenever practical, with drawings, approved submittals, physical conditions and materials available for review.

**How:**

- Review drawings and specifications
- Review approved submittals and samples
- Review RFIs, changes and coordination drawings
- Confirm permits and prerequisite inspections
- Confirm qualified personnel
- Confirm testing agency and special inspector
- Confirm materials, equipment and delivery status
- Inspect substrate and preceding work
- Confirm layout, dimensions and tolerances
- Confirm mockup and benchmark requirements
- Review manufacturer instructions
- Review installation sequence
- Review inspection and test plan
- Identify hold and witness points
- Confirm test equipment and calibration
- Confirm safety and environmental controls
- Identify likely conflicts and failure risks
- Resolve deficiencies before release
- Record meeting and approval to begin

### Initial phase — when, where and how

**When:** At the beginning of the first representative installation, first placement, first assembly, first test section or first shift of each definable feature.

**Where:** At the actual first-work location or approved representative mockup.

**How:**

- Verify preparatory requirements remain satisfied
- Inspect first materials and equipment
- Verify layout and dimensions
- Observe installation sequence
- Verify workmanship and tolerances
- Confirm test frequency and methods
- Compare work to mockup or sample
- Confirm safety and environmental controls
- Identify adjustments before repetitive work expands
- Correct first-work deficiencies
- Establish accepted benchmark
- Train crews to benchmark
- Record results and authorize continued production

Repeat the initial phase for new crews, new locations with materially different conditions, changes in methods or materials, long interruptions, or deteriorating workmanship.

### Follow-up phase — when, where and how

**When:** Daily, each shift, continuously or at the specified sampling frequency while the definable feature remains in progress.

**Where:** Across active work locations and representative completed areas, including off-site fabrication when required.

**How:**

- Compare ongoing work to approved benchmark
- Verify approved materials and lots
- Inspect workmanship and tolerances
- Verify required tests and sampling
- Verify corrections remain effective
- Monitor storage and protection
- Inspect interfaces and concealed work
- Record daily results
- Stop or correct nonconforming work
- Repeat testing and inspection as required
- Continue until the feature is complete and accepted

## Hold points — when and how

**When:** Before an irreversible step, concealment, loading, energization, startup, shipment or production release.

**How:**

- Identify hold point in the inspection and test plan
- Complete prerequisite checklist
- Provide required notice
- Stop work at the defined point
- Make work accessible
- Provide current approved documents
- Conduct inspection or test
- Record acceptance
- Release work in writing
- Correct and reinspect when rejected

Common hold points include subgrade acceptance, reinforcement before concrete, waterproofing before cover, in-wall and above-ceiling work before closure, pressure tests before insulation, firestopping before concealment, equipment before energization and life-safety systems before occupancy.

## Required schedule controls

- Link every definable feature to preparatory, initial and follow-up controls when applicable.
- Schedule quality activities before irreversible work.
- Identify exact locations and frequencies.
- Use measurable acceptance criteria.
- Assign one responsible person.
- Include testing, report turnaround and retesting.
- Preserve hold points and do not bypass required releases.
- Tie deficiencies to corrective work and affected successors.
- Repeat the initial phase when crews, methods or conditions materially change.
- Track quality at fabrication, delivery, storage and installation.
- Integrate quality control with safety, inspections, testing, commissioning and closeout.
- Preserve searchable evidence of acceptance.
