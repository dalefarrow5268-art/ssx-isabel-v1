# SSX Master Schedule Management Activities

Potential construction activities for planning, developing, reviewing, maintaining, analyzing, communicating and closing the project schedule from preconstruction through warranty.

> Apply the scheduling requirements in the contract and project procedures. A schedule should be a current operating plan tied to real scope, responsibility, location, resources, procurement, approvals and field conditions—not merely a reporting document.

## Core schedule questions

| Field | Required answer |
|---|---|
| What must happen? | Clearly defined scope, deliverable, decision, procurement step, field operation, inspection, test or milestone |
| Where? | Project, phase, building, floor, room, area, grid, elevation, system or workfront |
| Who owns it? | One responsible organization and accountable person |
| When? | Planned, forecast, actual, required and contractual dates |
| Why then? | Logic, access, resource, approval, material, weather, permit or operational constraint |
| What must happen first? | Physical, contractual, safety, quality, information, procurement or resource predecessor |
| What follows? | Work or milestone released by completion |
| How long? | Original, remaining and actual duration supported by quantities and production |
| What could prevent it? | Risk, decision, material, labor, access, change, inspection, weather or interface |
| How is completion proven? | Installed quantity, approved document, delivery, inspection, test, acceptance or signed milestone |

## Required schedule controls

- Use one consistent data date for each update.
- Base progress on observable evidence.
- Separate actual dates, current plan and forecast.
- Connect every activity with valid logic except approved project endpoints.
- Avoid hard constraints unless contractually or physically required.
- Make activity names specific enough to identify action and location.
- Tie durations to quantities, production and calendars.
- Track decisions, design, procurement, inspections and turnover—not only field installation.
- Explain critical-path and milestone changes every update.
- Preserve approved baselines and prior updates.
- Incorporate authorized changes without rewriting historical performance.
- Treat recovery as measurable commitments, not an optimistic finish date.
- Maintain a reliable as-built record throughout the project.
