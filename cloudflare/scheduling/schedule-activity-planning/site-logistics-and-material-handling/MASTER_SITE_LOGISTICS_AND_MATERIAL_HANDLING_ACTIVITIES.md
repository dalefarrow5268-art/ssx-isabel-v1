# SSX Master Site Logistics and Material Handling Activities

Potential construction schedule activities for planning and controlling access, traffic, deliveries, staging, storage, cranes, hoisting, horizontal movement, temporary routes, relocation and final restoration.

> Select only activities applicable to the site, construction sequence, permits, adjacent operations and contract. Logistics plans must be coordinated with safety, emergency access, temporary facilities, environmental controls, public protection and the current project schedule.

## Core logistics questions

| Field | Required answer |
|---|---|
| What moves or changes? | People, vehicles, materials, equipment, waste, barriers, routes, cranes, hoists or storage |
| From and to where? | Gate, street, dock, laydown area, floor, room, roof, shaft, workface or offsite facility |
| When? | Delivery window, shift, phase, road closure, crane pick, relocation or removal date |
| Who controls it? | Logistics manager, superintendent, trade, carrier, operator, rigger, flagger, security or authority |
| How? | Truck, forklift, telehandler, crane, hoist, elevator, cart, conveyor, pump or manual handling |
| What must be ready? | Permit, route, capacity, clearance, bearing, operator, rigging, protection, labor or receiver |
| What could conflict? | Public traffic, emergency access, workers, other deliveries, overhead work, utilities, weather or occupied operations |
| What proves completion? | Approved plan, reservation, inspection, delivery ticket, lift record, photograph, restored area or release |

## Standard logistics workflow

1. Survey site and surrounding constraints
2. Define construction phases and workfronts
3. Prepare logistics plan
4. Coordinate public and emergency access
5. Obtain permits and approvals
6. Establish gates, routes, docks and staging
7. Establish delivery reservation process
8. Verify route, load and unloading requirements
9. Inspect equipment and assign qualified personnel
10. Receive and inspect delivery
11. Move material to storage or workface
12. Protect, identify and preserve material
13. Relocate logistics systems as construction advances
14. Remove temporary facilities and routes
15. Restore affected property
16. Close permits and archive records

## Typical schedule logic

- Site survey → logistics plan
- Logistics plan → permits and setup
- Gate and route setup → mobilization and deliveries
- Delivery reservation → receiving and unloading
- Receiving → storage or workface movement
- Crane/hoist installation → vertical material flow
- Phase completion → logistics relocation
- Permanent access available → temporary-route removal
- Material movement complete → hoist/crane removal
- Temporary-system removal → restoration and closeout

## Required schedule controls

- Link logistics activities to the construction work they enable.
- Calculate delivery dates from workface need dates and storage limits.
- Assign exact gates, routes, time windows and receiving parties.
- Verify route and equipment capacity before shipment.
- Separate delivery, unloading, receiving, storage and movement to workface.
- Schedule permits, closures, escorts and public notices.
- Protect emergency access and required egress at all times.
- Include crane and hoist erection, inspection, maintenance, relocation and removal.
- Control double handling and excessive onsite inventory.
- Update logistics plans as the project phases.
- Coordinate public, occupied-facility and adjacent-property impacts.
- Schedule removal and full restoration of temporary logistics systems.
