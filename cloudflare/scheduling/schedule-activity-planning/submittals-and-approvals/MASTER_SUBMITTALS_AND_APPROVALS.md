# SSX Master Construction Submittals and Approvals

Potential contractor, subcontractor, supplier, fabricator, designer, owner, consultant, utility and AHJ submittal activities that may affect a construction schedule.

> Use only the submittals required by the drawings, specifications, contract, approved submittal register, permits, authorities having jurisdiction, utilities, owner, design team, commissioning plan and project-specific procurement requirements.

## Recommended SSX tracking fields

For each applicable submittal or approval, track:

- Project, phase, area, building and bid package
- CSI division and specification section
- Submittal number, revision and title
- Submittal type
- Responsible trade, preparer, supplier and fabricator
- Required reviewer, approver and final decision authority
- Contract requirement and governing document
- Required-on-site or installation date
- Procurement, fabrication and shipping lead times
- Back-calculated need-by dates
- Preparation start and planned submission date
- Internal contractor review duration
- Design-team review duration
- Owner, consultant, utility or AHJ review duration
- Planned and actual submission, return and resubmission dates
- Review status and disposition
- Linked RFI, substitution, change, procurement item and schedule activity
- Approval hold point and affected work
- Required sample, mockup, test data or delegated-design calculation
- Long-lead, critical-path and expediting flags
- Responsible person and aging days
- Final approved revision and attachment
- Closeout conversion requirement

## Standard submittal workflow activities

1. Identify contract-required submittal
2. Add item to submittal register
3. Assign responsible trade and preparer
4. Confirm specification, drawing and procurement requirements
5. Confirm required-on-site and installation dates
6. Calculate fabrication and procurement release date
7. Calculate approval need-by date
8. Prepare submittal
9. Perform subcontractor or supplier quality review
10. Coordinate dimensions, interfaces and field conditions
11. Complete delegated-design engineering, when required
12. Obtain required professional seal, certification or manufacturer review
13. Submit to general contractor or construction manager
14. Perform contractor compliance and coordination review
15. Return incomplete or noncompliant package for correction, when required
16. Submit coordinated package to design team
17. Route to architect, engineers, consultants, owner, commissioning authority, utility or AHJ
18. Answer reviewer questions and provide supplemental information
19. Receive review disposition
20. Distribute reviewed submittal to responsible parties
21. Revise and resubmit, when required
22. Obtain final approval or approval as noted
23. Release material or equipment for procurement and fabrication
24. Update procurement and delivery dates
25. Incorporate approved information into coordination drawings and field work
26. Verify installed work matches approved submittal
27. Archive approved record copy
28. Convert applicable information into closeout documents

## Common approval hold points

- Do not procure before product approval
- Do not fabricate before approved shop drawings
- Do not release custom finishes before owner selection
- Do not conceal work before required inspection
- Do not install until coordinated drawings are approved
- Do not proceed beyond mockup until benchmark approval
- Do not energize until utility and AHJ authorization
- Do not start equipment until startup prerequisites are approved
- Do not occupy until life-safety approvals and TCO or CO
- Do not close contract until record and closeout submittals are accepted

## Typical schedule logic

- Design information available → submittal preparation
- Field dimensions available → shop drawings
- Shop drawings → internal contractor review
- Contractor review → design-team review
- Design-team review → revise and resubmit or final approval
- Final approval → release for fabrication
- Release → fabrication
- Fabrication → factory testing
- Factory testing → shipping
- Shipping → delivery
- Delivery → installation
- Installation → field testing
- Testing → startup and commissioning
- Commissioning → owner training
- Training and closeout submittals → substantial completion or final acceptance

When partial approval is intentionally used, identify the exact portion released and preserve later approvals as separate activities.

## Required scheduling controls

- Back-calculate every approval date from installation and required-on-site dates.
- Include realistic preparation, coordination, review, revision, fabrication and shipping durations.
- Distinguish first submission, review, resubmission and final approval as separate activities when schedule-critical.
- Do not use one generic submittal activity to represent an entire trade when individual packages have different release dates.
- Tie long-lead submittals directly to procurement and delivery activities.
- Flag overdue decisions and approvals before they affect fabrication or installation.
- Preserve revision history and do not overwrite approved records.
- Confirm final approval status before releasing controlled work.
- Use project-specific contract review durations and notice requirements.
- Track only applicable submittals and approvals.
