# SSX Master Construction Utility-Company Activities

Potential electric, natural-gas, water, sanitary-sewer, stormwater, telecommunications, cable, fiber, district-energy and other utility-provider activities that may affect a construction project schedule.

> Utility responsibilities, processes, terminology and lead times vary widely. Confirm every activity with the actual service provider, municipality, utility authority, owner, design professionals and project agreements.

## Recommended SSX tracking fields

For every applicable utility activity, track:

- Utility type and service provider
- Utility account, application, work-order and design number
- Project, building, phase, service and meter
- Utility representative and contact information
- Owner, contractor and design-team coordinator
- Service territory and governing tariff or standard
- Required load, flow, demand, pressure and capacity
- Existing and proposed point of connection
- Utility-furnished and customer-furnished scope
- Required application, drawings and technical data
- Fees, deposits, contributions and refunds
- Easements, licenses and access rights
- Permits and third-party approvals
- Planned, promised, forecast and actual dates
- Utility engineering and procurement durations
- Customer prerequisite work
- Required inspections and tests
- Outage, tie-in, energization or activation window
- Meter and account-transfer requirements
- Status, aging, constraints and escalation owner
- Linked permit, procurement, installation, testing and occupancy activities
- Acceptance letter, service record and attachment

## Standard utility workflow

1. Identify utility providers and service territories
2. Request availability and capacity information
3. Establish utility contacts
4. Define temporary and permanent service requirements
5. Submit application and required technical data
6. Pay application or engineering fees
7. Attend utility coordination meeting
8. Utility completes capacity and load review
9. Utility identifies point of connection and requirements
10. Design team incorporates utility criteria
11. Submit customer design for utility review
12. Utility prepares service design and cost estimate
13. Owner reviews and accepts design
14. Execute service agreement
15. Pay deposits, contributions and connection fees
16. Grant easements and access rights
17. Obtain permits and third-party approvals
18. Utility procures long-lead equipment
19. Customer completes required civil and building work
20. Utility inspects customer work
21. Correct deficiencies
22. Utility completes its construction
23. Coordinate shutdown and tie-in
24. Complete pressure, flow, electrical or communication testing
25. Install meter and service equipment
26. Obtain utility and AHJ releases
27. Energize or activate service
28. Complete startup and commissioning
29. Transfer account to owner
30. Complete utility record documents and closeout

## Typical schedule logic

- Availability request → capacity confirmation
- Capacity confirmation → service application
- Application → utility design
- Utility design → agreement, fees and easements
- Agreement → utility equipment procurement
- Approved design → customer infrastructure
- Customer infrastructure → utility inspection
- Utility equipment and inspection → utility construction
- Utility construction → shutdown and tie-in
- Tie-in → meter and activation
- Activation → equipment startup and commissioning
- Acceptance → account transfer and closeout

## Common hold points

- No excavation near existing utility before locate and verification
- No utility construction before approved design and permits
- No utility release before agreements, fees and easements
- No concealment before utility inspection
- No tie-in before approved outage plan
- No meter set before required inspections and account setup
- No energization or activation before utility and AHJ releases
- No equipment startup before stable required utility service
- No temporary service removal before permanent service is accepted
- No utility closeout before restoration and records are complete

## Required schedule controls

- Begin utility coordination during early design.
- Track each service and meter independently.
- Use utility-confirmed dates and preserve written commitments.
- Separate application, design, agreements, easements, procurement, construction and activation.
- Link customer prerequisites directly to utility activities.
- Treat utility-furnished equipment as long-lead procurement.
- Include off-site upgrades and third-party approvals.
- Include realistic outage-notice and tie-in durations.
- Track temporary-service transitions.
- Tie activation to startup, commissioning and occupancy.
- Maintain escalation and temporary-service contingencies.
- Complete account transfers, restoration and record closeout.
