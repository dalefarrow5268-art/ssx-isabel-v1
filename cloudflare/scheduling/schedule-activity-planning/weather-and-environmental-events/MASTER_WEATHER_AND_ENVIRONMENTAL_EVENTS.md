# SSX Master Construction Weather and Environmental Events

Potential forecast, monitoring, preparedness, restriction, shutdown, protection, response, recovery and documentation activities related to weather, natural hazards and environmental conditions on a construction project.

> Thresholds must come from the project safety plan, contract, manufacturer requirements, engineered temporary works, governing authorities and actual site conditions. Do not use this master list as a substitute for professional judgment, emergency instructions or official warnings.

## Recommended SSX tracking fields

For every applicable event or activity, track:

- Project, location, phase, building, floor, area and work activity
- Event or condition type
- Forecast source and observation source
- Forecast issue time and affected period
- Measured temperature, wind, precipitation, humidity or other condition
- Alert, watch or warning classification
- Project-specific action threshold
- Responsible monitor and decision authority
- Trades, crews, equipment and materials affected
- Planned protective or response action
- Planned and actual suspension dates and times
- Work hours lost
- Calendar days affected
- Recovery and remediation duration
- Predecessors and successors affected
- Schedule, cost and productivity impact
- Required notice and documentation
- Photographs, logs, reports and official records
- Insurance, force-majeure or claim status
- Restart inspection and authorization
- Status and corrective action

## Standard weather-event workflow

1. Monitor forecast and official alerts
2. Identify exposed work, personnel, equipment and materials
3. Compare forecast with project-specific thresholds
4. Notify responsible parties
5. Adjust work plan and deliveries
6. Install or verify protective measures
7. Secure cranes, hoists, scaffolds and temporary works
8. Suspend affected work when required
9. Protect life safety and maintain emergency access
10. Record actual site conditions
11. Inspect during and after event when safe
12. Document damage and delay
13. Complete cleanup, drying, repair or remediation
14. Verify temporary and permanent work
15. Obtain required reinspections
16. Authorize restart
17. Resequence or recover schedule
18. Preserve contract and insurance records
19. Close event

## Common hold points

- No exposed work during conditions outside approved limits
- No crane or lift operation above applicable engineered or manufacturer limits
- No restart before post-event inspection
- No concealment of wet or damaged materials
- No electrical reenergization after flooding before inspection
- No concrete, masonry, coating, roofing or sealant work outside approved conditions
- No environmental discharge after a permit exceedance until corrected
- No entry into structurally suspect areas before professional release
- No assumption that forecast conditions equal actual site conditions
- No weather-delay claim without contemporaneous records

## Required schedule controls

- Use project-specific thresholds and official warnings.
- Track forecast and actual conditions separately.
- Link weather-sensitive work to realistic seasonal calendars.
- Include preparedness, shutdown, protection, inspection and recovery—not only lost time.
- Preserve daily contemporaneous documentation.
- Track partial productivity loss as well as full shutdowns.
- Reinspect temporary works and concealed materials after significant events.
- Add drying, curing, stabilization, remediation and retesting durations.
- Tie restart to responsible authorization.
- Update schedule logic and forecasts after every material event.
- Coordinate with the SSX Weather System when project-specific automation is developed.
