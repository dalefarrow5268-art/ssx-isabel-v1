# SSX Master Workforce and Labor Activities

Potential construction schedule activities for forecasting, recruiting, onboarding, assigning, supporting, monitoring and demobilizing the workforce from preconstruction through closeout.

> Select only activities applicable to the contract, project location, collective-bargaining agreements, owner requirements and applicable employment law. Protect personal, medical, background-check and other confidential employee information.

## Core workforce questions

| Field | Required answer |
|---|---|
| Who is needed? | Company, trade, classification, skill, certification and supervision |
| How many? | Planned crew, peak workforce, replacement reserve and actual workforce |
| Where? | Project, building, floor, area, workfront, fabrication shop or shift |
| When? | Mobilization, start, ramp-up, peak, shift change, reassignment and release dates |
| For what work? | Schedule activity, quantity, production target and milestone |
| What is required first? | Contract, orientation, badging, screening, training, certification, tools and access |
| What can prevent performance? | Shortage, absenteeism, turnover, fatigue, stacking, access, material, rework or low productivity |
| How is readiness proven? | Approved roster, credential, orientation record, assignment, attendance and production report |

## Standard workforce workflow

1. Determine scope, quantities and schedule
2. Develop labor-loaded forecast
3. Identify skills and credentials
4. Confirm labor source and availability
5. Recruit or assign workforce
6. Complete screening and employment requirements
7. Complete orientation, training and badging
8. Mobilize supervision and crews
9. Assign workfronts and production targets
10. Track attendance, hours and output
11. Resolve shortages, congestion and performance issues
12. Ramp crews up or down with the schedule
13. Transfer knowledge and complete closeout
14. Demobilize and close workforce records

## Typical schedule logic

- Scope and quantities → labor forecast
- Labor forecast → recruiting and assignment
- Assignment → screening, orientation and badging
- Worker readiness → crew mobilization
- Workfront readiness and crew mobilization → production
- Production trend → staffing adjustment
- Labor shortage → recovery action
- Phase completion → crew ramp-down
- Punch and closeout → trade release
- Badge recovery and final payroll → workforce closure

## Required schedule controls

- Link manpower forecasts to quantities, production rates and exact schedule activities.
- Mobilize supervision before production crews.
- Schedule screening, orientation, training and badging before the first shift.
- Track qualifications and expiration dates for safety-critical work.
- Compare planned manpower and production with actual results.
- Distinguish labor shortage from unavailable workfront, material or information.
- Include all support coverage needed for added shifts.
- Monitor congestion, fatigue, safety and quality when increasing labor or overtime.
- Preserve confidential workforce records outside general project reporting.
- Forecast ramp-up and ramp-down early.
- Recover access credentials during demobilization.
- Retain qualified contacts for warranty response.
